The Product
An AI-native operating system
for the commercial broker
Six integrated modules that replace every fragmented tool a broker currently pays for separately.
OM Generator
Input address + deal type. AI pulls comps, demographics, and property data to produce a branded PDF offering memorandum in under 60 seconds. Replaces 8–10 hours of manual work per deal.
BOV Tool
Three-approach broker opinion of value (income, sales comparison, cost) with weighted reconciliation and a branded 2-page PDF output. Replaces CoStar comp research + manual spreadsheet work.
AI Lead Gen Engine
AI identifies off-market prospects via ownership records, debt maturities, permit data, and entity lookups. Auto-drafts personalized outreach for each lead. Replaces hours of cold research.
Broker Network Marketplace
Principals (investors, operators) search for brokers by market and asset type. Brokers get qualified inbound deal flow. A real-time social layer that CRE has never had.
CRM + Deal Room
AI-enriched contact database with deal history, relationship scoring, and automated follow-up sequences. All documents for a listing live in one deal room — OMs, BOVs, executed LOIs, due diligence.
Underwriting Suite
One-click pro forma with market rent, cap rate, and IRR sensitivity. Asset-class aware (NNN industrial, flex, retail). Exports buyer-ready Excel with full audit trail.

The Network Effect
A two-sided marketplace
with a data flywheel
The broker platform and the principal network reinforce each other. This is what makes it defensible — not features.

Principal side (Hempstead Labs clients)

1
Investor needs a broker in Pleasanton, CA. Today: Google + cold call + 2-day wait. On platform: search by market, see ranked profiles, deal history, and response rate. One-click intro.
2
More principals using the platform → more broker search queries → brokers see tangible deal flow from being listed.
3
Every principal-broker match adds a data point: who transacts where, what assets, at what pricing. The dataset compounds.

Broker side (Native AI Brokerage members)

4
Brokers join for the economics (better split + lower software cost). They stay for the inbound deal flow the marketplace generates.
5
More brokers → better market coverage → more value to principals → more Labs signups → more search queries → loop tightens.
6
Proprietary transaction data becomes a moat: the platform knows more about who transacts what, where, and at what economics than any broker database that exists today.
Nobody has built this layer. Buildout stayed B2B-to-brokers only. CompStak built the data layer but not the marketplace. CoStar has the data but not the brokerage. This is the gap.

Go-To-Market
Four phases,
one clear flywheel
The GTM is sequenced to build proof before scale — one marquee deal, then a wave of broker recruiting, then the content engine that makes the brand self-perpetuating.
1
Seed with 5–10 anchor brokers (Months 1–3)
Start with producers who already have deal flow. Build the product around their actual workflows. Prove the OM generator saves them 8–10 hours per deal. These brokers become the case studies and recruiting assets for phase 3.
2
Close one marquee deal under the brand (Months 3–6)
Nothing legitimizes a brokerage like a transaction. Target a $5–20M industrial or retail deal in target markets. Use it to produce a full AI-generated OM as a proof of concept to share with prospects.
3
Targeted broker recruiting campaign (Months 4–12)
Target mid-market producers at M&M, NAI, and Lee & Associates doing $500K–2M GCI who are frustrated with their split. Run their personal P&L for them in the pitch — the math is overwhelming. Show, don't tell.
4
Content + thought leadership flywheel (Ongoing)
Publish AI-generated market reports, deal comps, and trend analyses publicly under the brand. This drives inbound for both brokers and clients, building brand equity without ad spend. The platform becomes a CRE media brand.

Key Risks
What has to be
true for this to work
Brand credibility cold start
Institutional sellers may be reluctant to list with an unknown brand. Mitigation: seed with 5–10 high-producing brokers who bring existing relationships and a track record. The first marquee deal is existential — it needs to happen in months 3–6.
Broker licensing + compliance overhead
Operating a brokerage requires a designated broker in each state, E&O insurance, and DRE compliance in every market. Manageable but real operational overhead. Budget $30–60K/year in legal and compliance before you're at meaningful scale.
Legacy shops improve their tech
CBRE, JLL, and M&M will eventually improve their AI tooling. The moat is the data flywheel, the split economics, and the cultural identity — not features. First-mover advantage in broker recruiting matters more than feature parity with incumbents.
Strategic decision: brokerage vs. SaaS
Running a licensed brokerage has compliance and headcount overhead. A pure SaaS play (license the tech to independent brokers without running the brokerage) is cleaner but loses the commission upside and the brand story. The hybrid path — lightweight brokerage arm + licensable platform — is the optimal structure but adds complexity.

Bottom Line
This is a real idea
with a structural wedge
The commission split model is genuinely broken and Compass proved in residential that you can disrupt it with better tech and better economics. The CRE version hasn't been built. The timing — with AI capable of replacing 90% of manual brokerage document work — has never been better.
The wedge
Brokers are already paying $2,500/month for worse tools. You walk in cost-neutral on day one, before the split improvement even enters the conversation.
The defensibility
The two-sided marketplace creates a network effect. The data flywheel creates a compounding moat. Neither exists at any competitor today.
The analogy
Compass did this in residential ($10B+ company). eXp did it in residential without the AI ($4B company). Nobody has done it in commercial real estate.
